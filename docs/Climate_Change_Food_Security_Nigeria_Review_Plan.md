# The Impact of Climate Change on Food Security in Nigeria: A Review of Empirical and Theoretical Evidence — Plan

A journal-article review that synthesizes empirical findings (quantitative + qualitative) and key theoretical lenses on how climate change affects food security in Nigeria, structured with a PRISMA-style search and a narrative/thematic synthesis. The paper anchors “food security” on the FAO four pillars (availability, access, utilization, stability) and interprets evidence through climate-risk/vulnerability and food-systems frameworks to produce clear policy and research implications.

## 1) Scope and research questions

### Primary research question
How does climate change (temperature trends, rainfall variability, extremes) affect food security outcomes in Nigeria?

### Sub-questions (map to FAO pillars)
- **Availability**: effects on crop/livestock/fisheries productivity, post-harvest losses
- **Access**: impacts on prices, incomes, livelihoods, market functioning
- **Utilization**: nutrition, dietary diversity, water/health burdens
- **Stability**: interannual volatility, shocks (flood/drought), conflict/displacement

### Scope decisions
- **Geography**: Nigeria; allow comparative studies only if Nigeria results are explicit
- **Time window (default)**: 2000–May 2026, with emphasis on the most recent ~10 years
- **Outputs**: narrative synthesis, evidence tables, conceptual framework figure, PRISMA diagram

## 2) Conceptual and theoretical framework

- **Food security framing**: FAO 4 pillars + food systems perspective (production–processing–distribution–consumption)
- **Climate risk framing**: hazard–exposure–vulnerability (IPCC risk concept) to interpret pathways
- **Livelihoods/vulnerability lens**: Sustainable Livelihoods Framework (assets, shocks, coping/adaptation) for household-level studies
- **Adaptation lens**: autonomous vs planned adaptation; risk management (irrigation, improved varieties, diversification, storage, insurance)
- **Optional complementary lens (use if common in the literature)**: political ecology / institutional capacity (governance, inequality)

## 3) Review methods (PRISMA-style systematic narrative review)

### Databases and sources (aim for at least 4–6)
- Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar
- AGRIS, CAB Abstracts (agriculture), PubMed (nutrition/health)
- AJOL (African Journals Online)
- **Grey literature (selective, clearly labeled)**: FAO, WFP, IFPRI, World Bank, IPCC; Nigeria’s NIMET/NBS/NAERLS where relevant

### Search string design (iterate; keep a search log)
Core string:

(Nigeria) AND ("climate change" OR warming OR temperature OR rainfall OR precipitation OR drought OR flood OR "extreme events" OR "rainfall variability") AND ("food security" OR hunger OR nutrition OR "dietary diversity" OR "food access" OR "food availability" OR "food prices" OR "crop yield" OR "agricultural productivity")

Add pathway terms as needed: livelihoods, markets, conflict, displacement, irrigation, adaptation.

### Inclusion criteria
- Climate variables/exposures explicitly analyzed
- At least one food-security outcome (pillar-aligned) or close proxy (yield, consumption, HFIAS/FIES, DDS, stunting/wasting where linked)
- Nigeria (national or subnational)
- Empirical quantitative, qualitative, mixed methods; plus well-cited theoretical/conceptual papers used in Nigeria context
- English language
- Peer-reviewed prioritized; include key reports only if they provide unique Nigeria data/synthesis

### Exclusion criteria
- Not Nigeria-specific results
- No climate component (only anecdotal “weather” without data)
- Purely technical agronomy without food-security linkage (unless it clearly maps to availability/stability)

### Screening workflow (PRISMA)
- De-duplicate → title/abstract screening → full-text screening
- If solo: two-pass approach (initial pass + verification pass a week later)
- Record reasons for exclusion at full-text stage

### Data extraction template (spreadsheet fields)
- Bibliographic: author/year/journal
- Study design: cross-sectional/panel/time series/RCM downscaling/qualitative case study
- Location: state/LGA, agro-ecological zone
- Climate exposure(s): temperature, rainfall variability, drought index, floods, etc.
- Outcomes: yield, prices, HFIAS/FIES, DDS, anthropometrics, etc.
- Methods: econometrics, remote sensing, surveys, interviews, etc.
- Key findings: direction/magnitude; heterogeneity (gender, livelihood, region)
- Adaptation/coping: strategies and evidence of effectiveness
- Limitations

### Quality appraisal (choose one and apply consistently)
- **MMAT** (Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool), or
- **JBI/CASP** checklists (separate tools for qualitative/quantitative)

Use appraisal to weight confidence (do not automatically exclude unless very low quality).

## 4) Evidence synthesis approach

### Descriptive mapping of the literature
- Count studies by year, region, agro-ecological zone, methods, and outcome type
- Identify heavily studied vs under-studied zones (e.g., Sahel/Sudan savanna vs coastal/Niger Delta)

### Pathway-based thematic synthesis (mechanisms)
- **Production channel**: yield/livestock/fisheries impacts via heat, rainfall timing, pests/diseases
- **Market/income channel**: prices, wage/income shocks, market access disruptions (roads/floods)
- **Health/water channel**: disease burden, WASH constraints affecting utilization
- **Shock/conflict channel**: displacement, farmer–herder conflict interactions (report carefully; avoid over-claiming causality)
- **Adaptation channel**: irrigation, diversification, improved seed, extension, storage, social protection

### Heterogeneity and vulnerability
- Gender; smallholders vs commercial; pastoralists vs crop farmers
- Region (North East/North West vs Middle Belt vs South)
- Household wealth and institutional access (credit, extension, insurance)

### Strength-of-evidence summary
- Add a compact confidence note per theme (high/medium/low) based on quality + consistency.

### Optional quantitative synthesis
- Only if a subset reports comparable elasticities/standardized outcomes; otherwise use effect-direction tables + narrative synthesis.

## 5) Manuscript structure (journal article)

1. **Title**
   - The Impact of Climate Change on Food Security in Nigeria: A Review of Empirical and Theoretical Evidence
2. **Abstract** (structured if the journal requires)
   - Background; Methods; Results; Implications
3. **Introduction**
   - Nigeria context + climate trends + why a review is needed
   - Objectives and contributions
4. **Conceptual framework**
   - Diagram: hazards → pathways → food-security pillars, moderated by vulnerability/adaptation
5. **Methods**
   - Search strategy; databases; dates; inclusion/exclusion
   - PRISMA screening
   - Extraction + appraisal
   - Synthesis approach
6. **Results**
   - PRISMA flow summary
   - Descriptive literature map
   - Thematic findings by pathways/pillars
   - Evidence on adaptation effectiveness and constraints
7. **Discussion**
   - Interpret patterns; reconcile conflicting findings
   - Policy implications (climate-smart agriculture, risk financing, early warning, infrastructure, social protection)
   - Research gaps (data/methods; under-studied regions/outcomes)
8. **Conclusion**
   - Key takeaways + actionable recommendations
9. **References** (+ optional Supplementary material)
   - Search strings, extraction sheet template, appraisal rubric

## 6) Planned figures and tables

- **Figure 1**: Conceptual framework (hazard → pathways → pillars; moderators)
- **Figure 2**: PRISMA flow diagram
- **Table 1**: Definitions and indicators mapped to FAO pillars (HFIAS/FIES/DDS/yield/price)
- **Table 2**: Study characteristics (region, design, exposure, outcome, key results)
- **Table 3**: Thematic synthesis summary (mechanism, evidence direction, confidence, policy note)

## 7) Writing timeline (4–6 weeks)

- **Week 1**: finalize scope + protocol; run searches; de-duplicate
- **Week 2**: title/abstract screening; full-text retrieval
- **Week 3**: full-text screening; data extraction; quality appraisal
- **Week 4**: draft Results (tables + themes) + Methods
- **Week 5**: draft Intro/Framework/Discussion; refine implications
- **Week 6**: revise; format references; journal formatting; consistency check

## 8) Verification checklist

1. Coverage: each FAO pillar addressed by at least one evidence theme and at least one Nigeria-focused empirical source
2. Methods: PRISMA flow numbers reconcile; exclusion reasons recorded
3. Consistency: each major claim in Results maps to an extracted study entry; avoid causal language beyond evidence
4. Journal readiness: abstract matches results; discussion distinguishes evidence vs recommendations; tables/references comply with target journal

## 9) Key decisions (report transparently)

- PRISMA-style systematic narrative review (not meta-analysis) due to heterogeneous outcomes/methods
- Grey literature used selectively when it adds unique Nigeria data or widely used framing
- Climate–conflict–food security links presented cautiously with uncertainty stated
